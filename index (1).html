How to Find Business Leads in Nigeria: 7 Free Methods That Work in 2026

A practical, step-by-step guide for freelancers, agencies and small businesses who want paying clients without spending on ads.

By Dave_d_dave · Updated November 7, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: The best free way to find business leads in Nigeria is to search Google Maps for businesses in your target city and niche, then contact the owner on WhatsApp with a short, personalised offer. Add LinkedIn, Instagram and referrals to keep your pipeline full.

Most Nigerian businesses are easy to reach but hard to impress. They get dozens of generic messages every week. The methods below work because they help you find the right businesses and approach them with something specific.

1. Google Maps: the best free lead database

Search for a category plus a location, for example "restaurants in Port Harcourt" or "pharmacies in Ikeja". Each listing usually shows a phone number, address, opening hours and reviews.

  • Look for gaps: no website, few photos, unanswered reviews or an outdated listing are all things you can fix for them.
  • Record your findings in a simple spreadsheet: business name, phone, problem you spotted, date contacted.
  • Prioritise active businesses with recent reviews, since they are still trading and more likely to spend.

2. WhatsApp: where Nigerian deals actually happen

WhatsApp is the default business channel in Nigeria. A short message with one clear observation usually beats a long email. Keep it under five lines, mention the business by name and ask one easy question.

3. LinkedIn: for larger companies and decision makers

Use LinkedIn when you sell to companies, not shops. Search by job title (for example "Operations Manager" or "Head of Marketing") plus "Nigeria", then send a short connection note before pitching. Comment on a few of their posts first so your name is familiar.

4. Instagram: ideal for fashion, food, beauty and retail

Many Nigerian small businesses run entirely on Instagram. Search relevant hashtags and location tags, find accounts with good products but weak visuals or slow replies, and send a friendly DM offering one specific improvement.

5. Referrals: the highest-converting source

After every successful job, ask: "Who else do you know that might need this?" Referred leads already trust you, so they close faster and haggle less. Offer a small thank-you for introductions.

6. Online communities and groups

Join Facebook groups, WhatsApp communities and Telegram channels where business owners ask for help. Answer questions genuinely for a few days before mentioning your service. Value first, pitch second.

7. Markets, trade fairs and offline networking

Physical presence still matters. Visit business clusters, attend trade fairs and meetups, and carry a simple card or a QR code linking to your portfolio. A five-minute conversation often replaces ten cold messages.

Outreach message template (copy and adjust)

Hello [Name], I came across [Business Name] on Google Maps and noticed [specific observation, e.g. "you have great reviews but no website"]. I help businesses like yours [specific benefit]. Would you be open to a quick 5-minute chat this week?

Always replace the brackets with real details. Personalisation is what separates you from spam.

Common lead generation mistakes

  • Sending the same copy-paste message to everyone.
  • Pitching your service before showing you understand their problem.
  • Giving up after one message instead of following up politely 2 to 3 times.
  • Not tracking who you contacted and when.
  • Targeting everyone instead of one niche you can serve well.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest way to find business leads in Nigeria?

Searching Google Maps for businesses in your target city and category is the fastest free method. Each listing usually shows a phone number, address and reviews you can use to qualify the lead.

Is WhatsApp good for B2B lead generation in Nigeria?

Yes. WhatsApp is the most widely used messaging app in Nigeria, so a short, personalised message there often gets a faster reply than email.

How many leads should I contact per day?

Start with 15 to 25 personalised messages a day. Quality and personalisation matter more than volume.

Do I need paid tools to generate leads in Nigeria?

No. Google Maps, WhatsApp, LinkedIn and Instagram are free and enough to land your first clients.

Final thoughts

Pick one niche, one city and two of the methods above. Contact a small batch of businesses every day, track the results and improve your message weekly. Consistency, not cleverness, is what fills a pipeline.
